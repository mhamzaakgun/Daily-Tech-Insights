# Daily Tech Digest - October 29, 2026
> **Today's signal:** Platform access, privacy, and open model releases are changing how people use online services.

## 🔴 1. Reddit moves toward a logged-in experience
Reddit announced a shift toward requiring users to log in for more of its content as search traffic changes.

**Why it matters:** Login requirements can change discovery, accessibility, and the relationship between platforms and search engines.

**Source:** [TechCrunch - Reddit shifts toward a logged-in experience](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/29/as-search-traffic-dries-up-reddit-announces-its-shifting-to-a-logged-in-experience/), October 29, 2026

## 🟡 2. Mistral releases model weights
Mistral released weights for its trillion-parameter model, with cybersecurity among the intended applications.

**Why it matters:** Available weights can support independent deployment and evaluation, though practical use depends on licensing and compute resources.

**Source:** [TechCrunch - Mistral releases model weights](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/29/mistral-releases-weights-for-its-trillion-parameter-ai-model-targeting-cybersecurity/), October 29, 2026

## 🟡 3. DoorDash announces Instacart acquisition
DoorDash agreed to acquire Instacart in a $17 billion deal. The agreement is a proposed transaction, not a completed merger.

**Why it matters:** Combining restaurant and grocery delivery could reshape competition in local commerce, subject to approvals.

**Source:** [TechCrunch - DoorDash to acquire Instacart](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/29/doordash-to-acquire-instacart-for-17b/), October 29, 2026
