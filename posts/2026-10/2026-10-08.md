# Daily Tech Digest - October 8, 2026
> **Today's signal:** Tech firms are giving AI agents more room to act while starting to address the risks that access creates.

## 🔴 1. Google brought a general-purpose agent to Workspace
**Category:** Enterprise AI  
**Impact:** High

**Why it matters:** An agent that can use email, files, and calendars may save time, but it also needs clear permissions and a way to review consequential actions.

**What to know:** Google announced Gemini Agent for work, a unified assistant intended to answer questions, create content, run code, and work across Workspace apps. Google described identity, authorization, sandboxing, and network controls as part of its governance approach.

**Source:** [Google Cloud Blog - Welcome to Gemini at Work 2026: Introducing the Gemini agent](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/welcome-to-gemini-at-work-2026), October 8, 2026

## 🟡 2. Anthropic opened a security program for critical infrastructure
**Category:** Cybersecurity  
**Impact:** High

**Why it matters:** Utilities and other infrastructure operators face aging systems and high costs for testing fixes. Carefully bounded AI support could help defenders find weaknesses, though deploying changes safely remains difficult.

**What to know:** Anthropic announced its Critical Infrastructure Defense Program, offering models, engineers, and threat research to operators protecting services such as power and water. It also introduced a free vulnerability-scanning service for open-source projects called OSS Scanner.

**Source:** [Axios - Anthropic’s new plan to protect critical infrastructure](https://www.axios.com/2026/10/08/anthropic-critical-infrastructure-cybersecurity), October 8, 2026

**Quick Takeaway:** Giving agents tools and infrastructure access makes permission design and human oversight part of the product.
