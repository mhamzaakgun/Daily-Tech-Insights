# Daily Tech Digest - October 18, 2026
> **Today's signal:** AI companies are facing questions about who benefits from their growth, while the Character.AI case shows how product risk and legal exposure can reshape a startup.

## 🔴 1. Anthropic commits company shares to a public-benefit fund
Anthropic said it will place 5% of its shares into a new Anthropic Public Benefit Fund. The company says the fund could support nonprofits or direct distributions to people affected by AI-driven labor-market changes.

**Why it matters:** The structure ties some potential company value to public-benefit work. Its eventual scale depends on Anthropic’s valuation and on how the fund makes and reports its decisions.

**Source:** [Anthropic - Investing in the public benefit](https://www.anthropic.com/news/investing-in-the-public-benefit), October 18, 2026

## 🔴 2. Disney plans to hire most of Character.AI’s workforce
A bankruptcy filing reported by TechCrunch says Disney offered jobs to at least 60 Character.AI employees, with the majority accepting. Disney had already agreed to acquire some assets for $20 million; the arrangement remains a “stalking horse” bid that another bidder could exceed.

**Why it matters:** Talent and technology can move to a larger company even as a startup enters bankruptcy. The filing also cites high operating costs and potential litigation liabilities, including scrutiny around child safety.

**Source:** [TechCrunch - Disney agrees to hire the majority of Character.AI’s workforce](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/18/disney-agrees-to-hire-the-majority-of-character-ais-workforce/), October 18, 2026

## 🟡 3. Anthropic’s draft AI bill sets a high intervention threshold
Tom’s Hardware reported on a draft proposal Anthropic has been discussing with lawmakers. The proposal would allow the federal government to block a model release only when it could cause at least 50 deaths or serious injuries, or $1 billion in damage.

**Why it matters:** The proposal illustrates the debate over how AI rules should balance innovation with precaution, and which agency should have authority to intervene. It is a company-backed draft, not enacted law.

**Source:** [Tom’s Hardware - Anthropic’s proposed AI safety bill](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/anthropics-proposed-ai-safety-bill-only-kicks-in-if-50-deaths-or-usd1-billion-in-damages-are-on-the-line-proposal-would-effectively-make-the-commerce-department-the-only-agency-capable-of-stopping-an-ai-model), October 18, 2026

**Quick Takeaway:** AI’s impact is increasingly being measured through institutions and obligations: who shares in the gains, who bears the risks, and what rules apply.
