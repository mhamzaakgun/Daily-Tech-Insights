# Daily Tech Digest - October 5, 2026
> **Today's signal:** AI transparency and basic data stewardship both faced practical tests.

## 🔴 1. OpenAI plans invisible text watermarks in the EU
**Category:** AI Policy  
**Impact:** High

**Why it matters:** A detectable mark could help platforms and researchers identify AI-generated text, but it is not proof of authorship and may weaken after editing.

**What to know:** OpenAI said it will add textGrain watermarks to eligible ChatGPT and Codex text for EU users. The method subtly shapes word choices so a detector can recognize a pattern; OpenAI says short or heavily edited text is harder to detect.

**Source:** [TechCrunch - OpenAI will start watermarking ChatGPT’s text in the EU](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/openai-will-start-watermarking-chatgpts-text-in-the-eu/), October 5, 2026

## 🔴 2. A breach exposed millions of Danish identity records
**Category:** Cybersecurity  
**Impact:** High

**Why it matters:** National identity databases concentrate information that can enable fraud and impersonation. The incident also shows how access granted to a third party can become a route into public systems.

**What to know:** Denmark confirmed that an attacker used a Danish company’s legitimate search access to reach its Central Person Register. TechCrunch reported that names, addresses, identity numbers, and other records affecting about 8 million people were taken.

**Source:** [TechCrunch - Hackers steal 8 million citizens’ records from Danish government database](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/hackers-steal-8-million-citizens-records-from-danish-government-database/), October 5, 2026

**Quick Takeaway:** AI labels need clear limits, and sensitive databases need strong oversight of every account that can query them.
