# Daily Tech Digest - October 17, 2026
> **Today's signal:** AI is changing how the web is made and discovered, while public institutions are weighing how much control to give technology vendors over sensitive systems.

## 🔴 1. AI-generated low-quality websites surged
Axios reported that domains it classifies as low-quality, AI-generated “slop” sites increased more than fivefold year over year in Q2 2026, while traffic to them quadrupled, citing ad-measurement firm DoubleVerify.

**Why it matters:** AI answer engines create new incentives to publish at scale. More pages do not necessarily mean more useful information, and search and recommendation systems need to distinguish original reporting from automated filler.

**Source:** [Axios technology coverage, October 17](https://www.axios.com/technology/2026/10/17) · [DoubleVerify](https://doubleverify.com/)

## 🟡 2. Publishers are drawing different lines for AI training and search
An Axios analysis published Saturday found that more than 40% of major U.S. publishers block AI training crawlers, compared with 25% that block AI search crawlers.

**Why it matters:** Publishers may want their work cited and discovered by AI search while restricting its use as training data. The distinction could shape future licensing, crawler rules, and how AI products attribute sources.

**Source:** [Axios technology coverage, October 17](https://www.axios.com/technology/2026/10/17)

## 🟡 3. NHS trusts push back on a patient-data platform
The Guardian reported that many NHS trusts in England had rejected Palantir’s patient-data technology, amid pressure on the government over its £330 million contract.

**Why it matters:** Large health-data platforms can support planning and care coordination, but adoption depends on public trust, clear safeguards, and confidence that sensitive records are being used appropriately.

**Source:** [The Guardian - Palantir patient data tech rejected by many NHS trusts in England](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/oct/17/palantir-patient-data-tech-rejected-by-many-nhs-trusts-in-england), October 17, 2026

**Quick Takeaway:** The day’s signal was governance: AI systems are changing information supply, and institutions still need to set clear limits for data use and deployment.
