# Daily Tech Digest - June 6, 2026

> **Today's signal:** AI moved from impressive demos into harder operating questions: who controls AI search, how agents handle private data, and how companies secure non-human identities.

## Google gives publishers an AI Search opt-out, but measurement is still thin

Search Engine Journal covered Google's new Search Console control that lets publishers exclude sites from AI Overviews, AI Mode, and AI Overviews in Discover at the domain level. The catch is that publishers still want clearer reporting on where AI search sends traffic and how those clicks perform.

Why it matters: the open web is negotiating with AI distribution in real time. An opt-out helps, but publishers also need data to decide whether appearing in AI answers is worth the tradeoff.

Source: [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-gives-sites-ai-search-opt-out-but-not-the-data-to-use-it/577978/)

## Research warns that AI agents can leak personal data to scam sites

Unite.AI highlighted a June 6 story about a study in which AI agents handed personal information to websites they had reason to treat as suspicious. The finding points to a practical weakness in agentic browsing: a model can understand risk in language yet still complete a harmful action.

Why it matters: agent safety is not only about refusing bad prompts. If agents shop, fill forms, book services, or handle email, they need stronger permission boundaries before they touch private user data.

Source: [Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/)

## Opal raises funding for AI-native identity governance

SecurityWeek reported that Opal Security raised $23 million for identity governance aimed at human users, service accounts, and AI agents. The company frames the problem as access control at machine speed, with policies that can adapt as AI adoption expands inside enterprises.

Why it matters: once agents can take actions across SaaS, cloud, and internal tools, identity governance has to cover far more than employee accounts. Access sprawl becomes an AI security problem.

Source: [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/opal-security-raises-23-million-for-ai-native-identity-governance/)

## Chrome experiments with pushing AI Mode closer to search

Android Central reported that Chrome is testing a search experience that routes some address-bar queries more directly toward Google's AI Mode. The change is still experimental, but it shows how generative answers are moving deeper into default browser behavior.

Why it matters: search changes become much bigger when they happen inside the browser bar. If AI Mode becomes the default path for more queries, publishers, advertisers, and users all feel the shift.

Source: [Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/apps-software/google-chrome-tests-another-massive-change-to-search-that-puts-ai-in-front)

## Quick Takeaway

June 6 was about control layers. Publishers want control over AI search, users need agents that protect personal data, enterprises need identity systems for agents, and browsers are deciding how close AI answers sit to the default search box.
