# Daily Tech Digest - June 26, 2026

> **Today's signal:** The day’s strongest stories all point to the same tension: technology is advancing quickly, but infrastructure, security, regulation, and human judgment still determine whether progress becomes useful.

## IBM's new chip architecture pushes below one nanometer

IBM demonstrated a 0.7-nanometer-class test chip built with a new “nanostack” transistor architecture. The company says the design can fit nearly 100 billion transistors onto a fingernail-sized chip and could deliver up to 50% higher performance or 70% better energy efficiency than its earlier 2 nm technology.

**Why it matters:** Traditional transistor scaling is running into physical limits. Stacking transistor structures vertically offers another path to denser and more efficient processors, although IBM says commercial production may still be several years away.

Source: [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/semiconductors/ibm-goes-sub-1nm-develops-0-7nm-class-technology-offering-up-to-50-percent-higher-performance-and-70-percent-higher-energy-efficiency-compared-to-ibms-2nm-class-node)

## Europe moves toward stricter rules for AWS and Azure

The European Commission reached a preliminary view that Amazon Web Services and Microsoft Azure should be designated as gatekeepers under the Digital Markets Act. Regulators cited their scale, entrenched customer bases, high switching costs, and importance to businesses building cloud and AI services.

**Why it matters:** A gatekeeper designation could force changes to cloud-platform practices even though AWS and Azure do not meet the law’s usual quantitative thresholds. The case may influence how Europe regulates infrastructure layers beneath the consumer internet.

Source: [ITPro](https://www.itpro.com/business/policy-and-legislation/aws-says-cloud-market-gatekeeper-designation-risks-deterring-European-investment-and-innovation-as-EU-regulators-plot-competition-crackdown)

## A third-party compromise drains Polymarket user wallets

Polymarket said attackers compromised an outside vendor and injected a malicious script into its frontend for some users. Blockchain researchers estimated that close to $3 million was taken, while the company said it removed the affected dependency and would fully reimburse impacted users.

**Why it matters:** Software supply-chain risk is not limited to libraries installed on servers. A compromised service that can alter browser-delivered code may reach users’ wallets directly, making vendor controls and frontend integrity critical security boundaries.

Source: [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/security/prediction-market-giant-polymarket-hit-by-cyberattack-with-company-confirming-user-funds-stolen-here-is-what-we-know)

## Ford brings experienced engineers back into its AI workflow

Ford reportedly rehired 350 experienced engineers after learning that automation could not replace the judgment needed for vehicle design, manufacturing, and software quality. The company combined returning expertise with expanded automated testing and tighter integration between traditional and software engineering teams.

**Why it matters:** AI can accelerate engineering work, but it depends on good data, review, and domain knowledge. Ford’s course correction is a practical example of augmentation working better than simply removing experienced people from complex systems.

Source: [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/ford-rehired-350-engineers-ai-quality-jd-power)

## Microsoft gives Windows 10 users another year of security updates

Microsoft extended the consumer Windows 10 Extended Security Updates program to October 12, 2027. PCs already enrolled will continue receiving critical security patches automatically, giving users more time before moving to Windows 11 or replacing incompatible hardware.

**Why it matters:** Operating-system transitions are also security and hardware-lifecycle problems. Extending patches reduces pressure for premature PC replacement and lowers the risk created by a large population of unsupported devices.

Source: [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/computing/windows/microsoft-extends-windows-10-support-out-of-the-blue-consumers-now-get-updates-for-another-year-to-october-2027)

## Quick Takeaway

Breakthrough hardware is only one part of technological progress. This day’s news shows that durable advances also require competitive infrastructure, safer dependencies, experienced people, and support timelines that match how technology is actually used.
