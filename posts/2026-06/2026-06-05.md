# Daily Tech Digest - June 5, 2026

> **Today's signal:** AI moved from product excitement into harder constraints: national security rules, semiconductor market stress, and the politics of who controls strategic technology projects.

## White House pushes faster AI adoption for national security

Reuters reported that the White House said it would accelerate the development and use of AI for national security applications, including intelligence and military domains, while saying the technology should not be used for unlawful surveillance.

Why it matters: government AI policy is becoming more operational. Instead of only debating model risk in the abstract, agencies are being pushed to adopt AI while also building guardrails around security testing, surveillance, and dependence on specific vendors.

Source: [Reuters via StreetInsider](https://www.streetinsider.com/Reuters/US%2Bsays%2Bit%2Bwill%2Bspeed%2Bdevelopment%2Band%2Buse%2Bof%2BAI%2Bfor%2Bnational%2Bsecurity/26611091.html)

## Chip selloff wipes out more than $1 trillion in market value

Reuters reported that U.S.-traded chipmakers dropped sharply on Friday, erasing more than $1 trillion in market value as Broadcom's weaker AI-chip outlook spilled into Nvidia, Micron, AMD, Marvell, and the broader semiconductor trade.

Why it matters: AI infrastructure is still a powerful growth story, but valuations are becoming less forgiving. When one major supplier disappoints, investors now reassess the whole chain from custom AI silicon to memory, networking, and server demand.

Source: [Reuters via WSAU](https://wsau.com/2026/06/05/chip-selloff-erases-over-1-trillion-in-stock-market-value/)

## ASML CEO warns Brussels not to over-direct strategic tech projects

Reuters reported that ASML CEO Christophe Fouquet welcomed much of the European Commission's technology-sovereignty push, but warned against Brussels steering or monitoring strategic projects too tightly. He argued that industry needs should drive which projects receive support.

Why it matters: Europe wants to close the tech gap with the U.S. and Asia, especially in chips and cloud infrastructure. The tension is how to use public policy to build capacity without burying strategic projects under bureaucracy.

Source: [Reuters via KFGO](https://kfgo.com/2026/06/05/asml-ceo-says-eu-commission-should-not-try-to-direct-strategic-projects/)

## Quick Takeaway

June 5 showed the physical and political side of AI. Governments want faster national-security adoption, markets punished overheated chip expectations, and Europe kept wrestling with how much state direction strategic technology really needs.
