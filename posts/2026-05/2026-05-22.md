# Daily Tech Digest - May 22, 2026

Today's signal: Today's strongest signal is that platform decisions are moving from product roadmaps into rules, devices, and privacy obligations.

## 1. Apple tries to narrow the Epic remedy to Epic

Category: Apps / Platform policy

Impact: Apple asked the U.S. Supreme Court to review a lower-court order and argued that Epic's case should not create App Store payment-link rules for every U.S. developer. TechCrunch says Apple's petition also challenges the contempt finding tied to fees on outside purchases.

Why it matters: This is not just another Apple/Epic filing. If Apple's argument works, developers may get a narrower path to alternative payments; if it fails, the App Store's U.S. payment rules keep shifting toward broader developer choice.

Source: TechCrunch - May 22, 2026: [Apple says Epic lawsuit should not reshape App Store rules for all developers](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/22/apple-says-epic-lawsuit-shouldnt-reshape-app-store-rules-for-all-developers/)

## 2. Google's AI glasses move from keynote idea to wearable workflow

Category: AI / Hardware

Impact: TechCrunch tested Google's Android XR display glasses prototype at I/O. The demo showed Gemini activation from the frame, photo capture with AI edits, live translation, Maps directions, and object identification, but also noted prototype limits around sound, latency, focus, and eye strain.

Why it matters: The interesting part is not that Google has another glasses demo; it is that AI assistance is being pushed into a hands-free interface where translation, navigation, camera use, and contextual search happen without pulling out a phone. That makes privacy, battery, latency, and ergonomics the real adoption tests.

Source: TechCrunch - May 22, 2026: [We tried Google's AI glasses and they're almost there](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/22/we-tried-googles-ai-glasses-and-theyre-almost-there/)

## 3. Trump Mobile confirms customer data was exposed

Category: Cybersecurity / Privacy

Impact: Trump Mobile confirmed that customer names, email addresses, mailing addresses, cell numbers, and order identifiers were exposed on the open internet. The company attributed the exposure to a third-party platform provider and said it was evaluating whether customer notifications are required.

Why it matters: The story is a reminder that "no network breach" is not the same thing as "no user harm." For young digital brands that outsource key infrastructure, third-party data handling can become the weakest part of the trust chain.

Source: TechCrunch - May 22, 2026: [Trump Mobile confirms it exposed customers' personal data, including phone numbers and home addresses](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/22/trump-mobile-confirms-it-exposed-customers-personal-data-including-phone-numbers-and-home-addresses/)

## Quick Takeaway

Today's theme is control surfaces: Apple is fighting over who controls app payments, Google is testing whether AI can control more of the ambient computing layer, and Trump Mobile shows how quickly customer trust can be damaged when data exposure sits outside the core product.
