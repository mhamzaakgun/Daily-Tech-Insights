# Daily Tech Digest - May 23, 2026

## 🔴 1. Starship V3 completes a mostly successful first flight
**Category:** Space / Infrastructure  
**Impact:** High  
**Why it matters:** SpaceX still needs repeatable heavy-lift capability for Starlink expansion and NASA's Artemis lunar architecture, so each Starship test changes the timeline for both commercial and public space programs.  
**What to know:** Ars Technica reported that the upgraded Starship V3 reached its planned trajectory and splashed down on target, while SpaceX still skipped a planned in-space engine relight after an engine issue. The useful signal is mixed but positive: the vehicle showed progress on heat-shield and payload-deployment goals, while propulsion reliability remains an engineering gate.  
**Source:** [Ars Technica - SpaceX's Starship V3--still a work in progress--mostly successful on first flight](https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/05/spacexs-starship-v3-still-a-work-in-progress-mostly-successful-on-first-flight/), published May 23, 2026

## 🔴 2. Anthropic says Mythos has already found more than 10,000 vulnerabilities
**Category:** AI / Cybersecurity  
**Impact:** High  
**Why it matters:** Security-focused AI is becoming less theoretical: major labs are now testing models as tools for finding real vulnerabilities across partner software.  
**What to know:** Engadget reported that Anthropic's Project Glasswing, powered by Claude Mythos Preview, had helped partners find more than 10,000 vulnerabilities within roughly a month of launch. The important question is not only how many bugs AI can find, but how teams validate, prioritize, and patch them without creating a new flood of unaudited findings.  
**Source:** [Engadget via Yahoo Tech - Anthropic Says Mythos Has Already Found More Than 10,000 Vulnerabilities](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/claude/articles/anthropic-says-mythos-already-found-124803872.html), published May 23, 2026

## 🔴 3. AI companion pets test the boundary between robotics and emotional products
**Category:** AI / Consumer hardware  
**Impact:** Medium  
**Why it matters:** The next consumer AI wave may not arrive only as chat apps; it may come as devices designed to build emotional attachment.  
**What to know:** Salon covered the Familiar, an AI-enabled domestic companion from Roomba co-founder Colin Angle. The product points to a broader market question: whether people want AI companions that learn household routines and social patterns, or whether privacy, usefulness, and emotional ambiguity will limit adoption.  
**Source:** [Salon - AI-enabled pets are here. Does anyone want them?](https://www.salon.com/2026/05/23/ai-enabled-pets-are-here-does-anyone-want-them/), published May 23, 2026
