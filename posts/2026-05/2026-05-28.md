## 🔴 3. Research suggests LLMs may learn falsehoods even when warned they are false
**Category:** AI research / Model reliability  
**Impact:** High  
**Why it matters:** If models absorb false claims from training data despite explicit warnings, data curation becomes more complex than simply labeling bad examples.  
**What to know:** Ars Technica covered research on "negation neglect," where LLMs fine-tuned on documents containing false claims still showed belief in those claims even when the documents warned that they were false. For builders, the takeaway is practical: how information is phrased and structured in training data can matter as much as whether it is labeled.  
**Source:** [Ars Technica - LLMs believe false statements even after explicit warnings that they're false](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/05/llms-believe-false-statements-even-after-explicit-warnings-that-theyre-false/), published May 28, 2026

## Quick Takeaway
May 28 was a builder's day: AWS, Figma, and AI researchers all pointed to the same lesson. Agents need better infrastructure, but they also need stronger controls around how work is changed, reviewed, and remembered.
