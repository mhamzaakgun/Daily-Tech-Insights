# Daily Tech Digest - May 24, 2026

Today's signal: The AI boom is no longer just a model race. Its economics are spilling into API pricing, memory supply chains, and the reliability expectations people have for the devices they already own.

## 1. DeepSeek makes its V4 Pro price cut permanent

Category: AI / Model economics

Impact: The Next Web reports that DeepSeek made its 75% V4 Pro discount permanent, cutting output pricing to $0.87 per million tokens and positioning the model below major Western rivals on price. The article frames the move as another step toward lower-margin AI infrastructure.

Why it matters: Cheaper long-context models can make document analysis, codebase review, and high-volume automation more affordable. But the buying decision is not only price; enterprises still have to weigh reliability, data sensitivity, geopolitics, and unresolved claims about model training provenance.

## 2. AI data centers squeeze the cheap smartphone market

Category: Hardware / AI infrastructure

Impact: The Next Web says memory makers are reallocating DRAM wafer capacity toward high-bandwidth memory for AI data centers, contributing to steep LPDDR price increases. The article says India's sub-$100 smartphone market fell 59% year over year in Q1 2026, while budget device makers face a harsher component-cost environment.

Why it matters: AI infrastructure has second-order effects. When hyperscalers outbid consumer-device makers for memory, the cost of the AI boom can show up as more expensive phones and weaker access to computing in lower-income markets.

Source: The Next Web - May 24, 2026: [AI is killing the cheap smartphone. The memory that powers your phone now goes to data centres instead.](https://thenextweb.com/news/ai-killing-cheap-smartphone-dram-memory-crisis)

## 3. HP's critical BIOS update reportedly bricks premium laptops

Category: Software reliability / Hardware

Impact: The Next Web reports that HP BIOS updates delivered through Windows Update have left some ZBook Ultra G1a and EliteBook X G1a laptops unbootable. The affected updates were marked critical, and recovery can require a specific HP USB-C Ethernet dongle or other support steps.

Why it matters: Firmware updates sit below the operating system, so a bad automatic update can turn a security-maintenance channel into a hardware failure. For developers and students, it is a reminder that update pipelines need staged rollout, rollback paths, and clear user control when the blast radius is a machine that will not boot.

Source: The Next Web - May 24, 2026: [HP pushed a critical BIOS update through Windows Update. It bricked its most expensive laptops.](https://thenextweb.com/news/hp-bios-update-bricking-premium-laptops-zbook-elitebook)

## Quick Takeaway

May 24's theme is pressure moving down the stack. AI price cuts make models cheaper at the API layer, AI data-center demand makes consumer memory more expensive, and firmware-update mistakes show how fragile everyday computing can become when low-level systems fail.
