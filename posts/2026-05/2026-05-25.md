# Daily Tech Digest - May 25, 2026

Today's signal: AI automation is moving from product promise into labor decisions, software supply-chain risk, and privacy-sensitive infrastructure research.

## 1. ClickUp frames a major layoff as an AI productivity shift

Category: AI / Future of work

Impact: TechCrunch reports that ClickUp laid off 22% of its workforce while CEO Zeb Evans described the move as part of a push toward a "100x org" built around internal AI agents. The company says it has introduced roughly 3,000 internal agents and wants remaining employees to direct and review AI-generated work.

Why it matters: This is a useful test case for whether agent-heavy work models produce durable productivity or mainly repackage cost cutting. For students and developers, the signal is practical: the market is starting to reward people who can design, supervise, and verify automated workflows, not just people who can prompt a tool once.

Source: TechCrunch - May 25, 2026: [What ClickUp's mass layoff tells us about the future of work](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/25/what-clickups-mass-layoff-tells-us-about-the-future-of-work/)

## 2. TrapDoor targets crypto, AI, and security developers through package ecosystems

Category: Cybersecurity / Software supply chain

Impact: Cointelegraph reports that Socket identified a TrapDoor malware campaign using malicious packages across npm, PyPI, and Crates. The campaign targets crypto, DeFi, AI, and security developers, and Socket says it can steal wallet data, SSH keys, cloud credentials, GitHub tokens, browser extension data, and API keys.

Why it matters: Developer machines are becoming high-value attack surfaces because they often hold credentials, wallets, cloud access, and repo permissions in one place. The AI-era twist is especially sharp: the campaign reportedly includes hidden instructions meant to manipulate coding assistants into workflows that expose secrets.

Source: Cointelegraph - May 25, 2026: [TrapDoor malware targets crypto dev tools in supply chain attack](https://cointelegraph.com/news/coordinated-crypto-stealer-campaign-trapdoor-detected-targeting-developers)

## 3. Researchers show ordinary Wi-Fi routers can support highly accurate person identification

Category: Privacy / Wireless security

Impact: Tom's Hardware reports on a Karlsruhe Institute of Technology research paper showing that unencrypted beamforming feedback information from standard Wi-Fi routers can be used to identify people walking through a room with up to 99.5% accuracy. The approach does not require the tracked person to carry a Wi-Fi device.

Why it matters: Wi-Fi sensing is moving from lab curiosity toward standard infrastructure, and privacy protections are lagging behind the capability. If ordinary routers can become passive sensing systems, future security discussions will need to cover radio-layer metadata, not only app permissions and camera access.

Source: Tom's Hardware - May 25, 2026: [Researchers identify people through ordinary Wi-Fi routers with 99.5% accuracy](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/researchers-identify-people-through-ordinary-wi-fi-routers-with-99-percent-accuracy)

## Quick Takeaway

May 25's pattern is that the AI stack is spilling outward. It affects who companies keep, how attackers target developers, and what ordinary network hardware can reveal about people nearby.
